## Cohort and rollout boundary

Migration `0029_member_booking_pilot_cohort.sql` adds a person-scoped active/revoked cohort and append-only actor/reason events. This uses stable `personId`, so eligibility follows the human identity rather than a client facet. The authenticated Member sees only `{ enabled: boolean }`.

`PILOT_MEMBER_AUTH_ENABLED` controls local Member session authentication. The separate `PILOT_MEMBER_BOOKING_ENABLED` flag defaults off; startup rejects enabling booking unless Member pilot auth is enabled in development/test. Admin cohort management has its own development/test-only flag and `member.pilot.booking.manage` permission. The local launcher sets these flags only on the loopback processes. The deterministic synthetic seed enrolls Member A only; Member B remains outside the cohort and seed reruns do not re-enroll a revoked member.

All four self-service mutations resolve the server-side session and then check the flag plus the current cohort row on every request: reserve, join waitlist, cancel reservation, and leave waitlist. A 403 `member_pilot_action_not_enabled` fails closed and takes effect immediately after revocation. Member reads remain available. Existing calendar, eligibility, quota, capacity, cancellation, waitlist promotion, idempotency, audit and notification outbox logic is reused.

The Member UI keeps browsing, profile, membership and booking history available outside the cohort, disables booking controls and displays “Las reservas desde la app todavía no están activadas para tu cuenta.” A revoked member's next rejected mutation also disables local booking actions. Preview still uses fictional fixture data and cannot mutate.

## Synthetic acceptance and browser smoke

On a disposable local PostgreSQL database, Member A's integration acceptance passed: available session and eligibility; reservation; same-key replay without duplicate reservation or quota use; occupancy and entitlement decrement; notification and audit; normal-window cancellation with entitlement restored, occupancy reduced, notification and audit; waitlist join/replay/leave; waitlist promotion through the existing staff cancellation path with exactly one entitlement consumption and promotion notification. The synthetic service was temporarily configured to the existing `consume` late-cancellation policy; its preview reported no restoration and the quota stayed consumed, after which the original policy was restored in `finally`.

Member B authenticated and read profile/session data but remained ineligible to book. Direct reservation returned 403. Cohort Member A's attempts to cancel Member B's seeded reservation and leave Member B's waitlist entry both returned 404 and left those records unchanged. A separate revocation test proves all four mutations fail immediately without logout while profile, membership, sessions and bookings remain readable. Flag-off reads remain available and mutations return 403. Admin can explicitly enable/revoke with a reason; Trainer is denied cohort management. Existing 2J-084 tests continue to cover identity isolation and resolved-route CSRF; 2J-085 adds reservation-path wrong-Origin, allowed-Origin and Referer-fallback regression coverage.

The real browser smoke used synthetic accounts on loopback only. On the normal desktop viewport (~1264×713), Member A signed in, reserved a session, viewed the reservation, cancelled it, joined and left the full session's waitlist, and saw the corresponding in-app reservation/cancellation/waitlist notifications. At 390×844, Member B browsed sessions and saw the clear pilot message with actions unavailable. A direct Member B reservation request returned 403 `member_pilot_action_not_enabled`. The local Admin Command Center loaded with occupancy data; the Trainer home loaded its agenda and timeclock entry point. No clock-in record was created. The existing worker initially exposed a Windows `Date` parameter serialization failure; timestamp bindings in reminder scheduling/outbox dispatch now use ISO timestamps. The worker delivered the booking-related notifications in the browser smoke, and the PostgreSQL notification-worker regression passed.
